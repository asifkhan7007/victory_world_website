Blackjack Rules and Card Values

The goal is to finish with a hand closer to 21 than the dealer without exceeding 21. Going over 21 is a bust and normally ends that hand immediately.

CardValue
2 through 10Printed face value
Jack, Queen and King10
Ace1 or 11, whichever creates the useful valid total

Natural Blackjack

An initial two-card hand containing an ace and a ten-value card is a natural Blackjack. The result and payout shown by the table are authoritative.

Dealer rules: The dealer completes the hand automatically according to the displayed table rules. Do not assume a particular soft-17 rule or payout unless the table shows it.

KNOW THE CONTROLS

Hit, Stand, Double Down and Split

Hit

Take another card. You may continue while the hand is active, but exceeding 21 busts the hand.

Stand

Keep the current total and end your decisions for that hand.

Double Down

When available, increase the wager and receive the table-defined final card for the hand. Use the enabled control and displayed rules.

Split

When available for the starting pair, divide it into two hands and play each separately. Pair eligibility and special split restrictions follow the table controls.

Simple Blackjack Strategy Guide

This is general learning guidance, not a guarantee. Exact basic strategy depends on the dealer's visible card and the table rules.

Your handBeginner ideaWhy
Hard 8 or lessUsually HitOne additional card cannot bust this total.
Hard 9 to 11Hit or Double when appropriateThese totals have useful improvement potential; Double depends on the dealer card and table rules.
Hard 12 to 16Compare carefully with dealer cardThese are the difficult totals: both hitting and standing can carry risk.
Hard 17 or moreUsually StandThe bust risk from another card is high.
Soft hand containing an aceUse the ace's flexibilityThe ace can change from 11 to 1 to avoid a bust.
Starting pairConsider Split only when availableCommon strategy often splits aces and eights, while tens and fives are usually kept together; exact play depends on rules and dealer card.

Common mistakes

  • Treating every 12 through 16 total the same without checking the dealer card.
  • Taking unnecessary cards on strong totals.
  • Doubling or splitting simply because the control is available.
  • Forgetting that a soft ace may change from 11 to 1.
  • Ignoring the turn timer instead of planning the next decision early.

Blackjack FAQ

What is the goal of Blackjack?

Finish closer to 21 than the dealer without exceeding 21.

How much is an ace worth?

An ace counts as 1 or 11, whichever produces the useful valid total.

What is a natural Blackjack?

It is an initial ace plus a ten-value card.

When can I split?

Split is available when the table permits the starting pair to become two hands. Follow the enabled control and displayed rules.
