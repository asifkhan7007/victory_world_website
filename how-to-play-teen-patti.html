Teen Patti Basic Rules

Teen Patti, sometimes called Indian Flush, is played with a standard 52-card deck. Each player receives three face-down cards. Players contribute to a shared pot and take turns deciding whether to continue or leave the hand. The last remaining player wins without a comparison, or the strongest eligible hand wins when cards are shown.

Objective

Win the pot by holding the stronger three-card hand at a show or by remaining after every opponent has packed.

Boot amount

The boot is the starting contribution placed into the pot by each participating player before cards are dealt. Victory World uses virtual chips with no cash value.

Blind and seen players

A blind player has not viewed the cards. A seen player has looked at them. The available stake and comparison actions can differ according to the player's current state and the table rules shown in the game.

Remember: Teen Patti rules can vary between tables. Always follow the controls, stake requirements and action availability displayed on your Victory World table.

STEP BY STEP

How a Teen Patti Hand Works

  1. Choose how to play. Select Practice, Create Private Room, Join With Code or Public Online.
  2. Choose the table settings. Select an available boot amount and turn time before joining or creating the table.
  3. Start the pot. Participating players contribute the boot amount.
  4. Receive three cards. Decide whether to remain blind or view your cards.
  5. Take your turn. Choose an available action before the turn timer expires.
  6. Finish the hand. The last remaining player wins, or cards are compared during a valid show.

Common actions

  • Blind: Continue without looking at your cards.
  • Chaal: Continue after viewing your cards by contributing the required amount.
  • Pack: Fold and leave the current hand.
  • Side Show: When permitted, request a private comparison with the previous eligible seen player.
  • Show: When permitted, request the final comparison that decides the winner.

PLAY SMARTER

Beginner Teen Patti Tips

  • Learn the six hand categories before concentrating on advanced decisions.
  • Use Practice mode to understand Blind, Chaal, Pack, Side Show and Show without risking anything of value.
  • Do not continue every hand simply because you have already contributed chips.
  • Watch the pot, your remaining virtual-chip balance and the current contribution required.
  • Make decisions before the 30-second or 70-second turn timer expires.
  • Remember that a confident opponent can still hold a weak hand; focus on both probability and betting behavior.

Teen Patti FAQ

How many cards does each Teen Patti player receive?

Each player receives three cards.

What is the highest Teen Patti hand?

A trail, also called three of a kind or a trio, is normally the highest hand category.

What is the difference between blind and seen play?

A blind player continues without viewing the cards. A seen player has viewed the cards and follows the applicable seen-player stake rules.

Can I practice Teen Patti for free?

Yes. Victory World Practice mode uses free virtual chips with no cash value.

Can I play Teen Patti with friends?
